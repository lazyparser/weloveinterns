This page is not a second position list: it is what the "open positions list" section of `open-internships.md` in the repository root looks like. When someone adds a position to the repository, a card will eventually appear here too. **Whether a position is still open is decided by the repository**; this page just saves you scrolling through 400 lines of markdown.

The list mixes three states: **open**, **details to come** (the repository entry still says TBA) and **filled**. Filled ones come last; they are not deleted — filled does not mean gone, and it may open again next year.

## How to apply

Email **Mr Wu at wuwei@rv2036.org**, following this format:

- **Subject**: **position number - name - phone number - school**; if you have already graduated, replace "school" with **part-time** (current student status is basically required)
- **Body**: a **self-introduction relevant to the position**, no more than 300 words
- **A PDF CV is mandatory** — emails without one are not guaranteed a reply

Before writing, it is worth reading three pieces: "Minimalist Project Management" (how PLCT and the joint programme are run), "How we recruit interns" and "How we grade and train interns". The links are all in the same section of the repository.

## Every position requires

- A strong drive to improve, wanting to become stronger and better
- Able to work with others actively and humbly, and to respond to teammates' messages promptly

Technical positions (development, testing, teaching assistant) additionally require:

- By default **LV2 or above** (a few positions relax this to LV1 — see each card)
- Fluent with the Linux command line and able to write simple bash scripts; comfortable using git for team collaboration
- A basic understanding of networking: able to set up SSH to any port, port forwarding, reverse connections and ProxyCommand yourself

## A few easily overlooked points

- The open-source community is **where you debut as an individual**; everything you do is public, so you need a strong heart
- The first internship contract is for three months, and you can join or leave at any time; the internship certificate runs from the contract's effective date to the actual date of departure
- When it ends you are free to negotiate a renewal with a member company; this internship places no constraint on later internship contracts
- **Positions with "no stipend" really exist**: positions such as K181–K183, funded by the lead himself, pay no stipend but provide a RISC-V development board and Token; the contract and the internship certificate are as usual

To understand how capability levels are graded and how you are assessed during the internship, see [how the joint programme works](program/); to see where the people who took this road are now, see [the intern list](interns/).
